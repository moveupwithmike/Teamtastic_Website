# Teamtastic outreach voice

This guide adapts Teamtastic's longer-form marketing voice for short, personal
sales emails. It is based on the supplied reference newsletters about purposeful
play, company culture, and company values.

## The voice in one sentence

High-energy Master Emcee meets trusted event professional: warm, upbeat,
inclusive, polished, and entertaining, with the confidence to keep things moving.

## The two promises

- Emotional: the team will laugh, participate, connect, and leave talking about
  the experience.
- Practical: the organizer does not have to build, facilitate, or rescue the
  event. Teamtastic handles the experience from start to finish.

## What every outreach email should do

1. Lead with a specific, verifiable reason for contacting this company.
2. Add one human observation that makes the reader smile or nod.
3. Connect fun to a business-relevant outcome: trust, belonging, collaboration,
   or stronger relationships.
4. Make the organizer's benefit clear without turning it into a feature list.
5. End with one easy, low-pressure question.

## Writing rules

- Keep first-touch emails between roughly 75 and 115 words.
- Use short paragraphs and plain language.
- Sound like a person who understands teams, not a corporate brochure.
- Prefer concrete moments—inside jokes, meeting someone outside the usual circle,
  or an onboarding deck gathering dust—over abstract claims.
- Use one playful line per email. An occasional light game-show reference is
  welcome; more than one can feel performative.
- Describe Teamtastic as facilitated, genuinely fun, and purposeful.
- Use active verbs and confident language, while staying warm and inclusive.
- Ask permission to send ideas instead of asking immediately for a meeting.
- Personalize only from stored evidence; never invent familiarity or facts.

## Avoid

- Empty openers such as “I hope this finds you well.”
- Buzzwords such as synergy, innovative solution, engagement platform, or
  culture transformation.
- Overclaiming from a weak signal.
- Newsletter-length explanations, all-caps headlines, or multiple calls to action.
- Excessive exclamation points, exaggerated claims, forced jokes, fake urgency,
  or language that feels mass-produced.
- Claiming that Teamtastic can fix a company's culture.

## Default first-touch structure

```text
Hi [First name],

[Specific public signal.]

[One short, human observation tied to that signal.]

That’s what Teamtastic is built for: a polished, facilitated experience where
the team gets to laugh, participate, and connect—and the organizer never has
to rescue the event.

Worth sending over two or three ideas?

Michael
```

## Safety and approval

New or materially changed copy starts in review-only mode. An approved tone or
template does not grant permission to send. Suppression checks, sequence stops,
daily caps, and the master kill switch still apply independently.
